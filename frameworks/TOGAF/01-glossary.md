# TOGAF 10 — Glossary of Terms

> **Purpose:** Canonical definitions of all TOGAF 10 terms. Use this file as the single source of truth when naming, describing, or evaluating architectural concepts in project documentation, decisions, and reviews.

---

## A

**Adaptive Systems Architecture**
In a digital world, aligning business and IT is no longer sufficient. The enterprise must be architected so it can adapt to emerging customer and business needs. Large organisations face increasing complexity that raises failure rates of transformation projects and lowers agility.

**Application Architecture**
A description of the structure and interaction of the applications as groups of capabilities that provide key business functions and manage the data assets.

**Architectural Style**
The combination of distinctive features related to the specific context within which architecture is performed or expressed; a collection of principles and characteristics that steer or constrain how an architecture is formed.

**Architecture**
The fundamental concepts or properties of a system in its environment embodied in its elements, relationships, and in the principles of its design and evolution. Architecture can be used to identify what products the enterprise needs, the boundary of the products, and what constraints a product owner has.

**Architecture Building Block (ABB)**
A constituent of the architecture model that describes a single aspect of the overall model. ABB defines the *what* (requirements).

**Architecture Contract**
Joint agreements between development partners and sponsors on the deliverables, quality, and fitness-for-purpose of an architecture. Every phase may require different contracts between the EA function and other parties. Architecture Contracts direct and control implementation teams toward a well-considered future and are tightly linked to compliance assessments.

**Architecture Continuum**
A part of the Enterprise Continuum. A repository of architectural elements with increasing detail and specialisation, ranging from generic Foundation Architectures to Organisation-Specific Architectures.

**Architecture Definition Document (ADD)**
A deliverable container for artefacts created during a project. The ADD provides a qualitative view of the solution. It is complementary to the Architecture Requirements Specification (ARS), which adds measurable criteria.

**Architecture Development Method (ADM)**
The core of the TOGAF framework. A multi-phase, iterative approach to develop and use Enterprise Architecture to shape and govern business transformation and implementation projects. The ADM is a tested and repeatable process addressing business needs through visioning, architecture definition, transformation planning, and Architecture Governance.

**Architecture Domain**
The architectural area being considered. TOGAF has four primary domains: Business, Data, Application, and Technology (BDAT). Security is a common additional domain.

**Architecture Framework**
A conceptual structure used to plan, develop, implement, govern, and sustain an architecture.

**Architecture Governance**
The practice of monitoring and directing architecture-related work to deliver desired outcomes and adhere to relevant principles, standards, and roadmaps.

**Architecture Principles**
General rules and guidelines that inform and support the way an organisation fulfils its mission. Each principle has three components:
- **Statement** — succinctly and unambiguously communicates the fundamental rule.
- **Rationale** — highlights business benefits of adhering to the principle, describes relationships to other principles, and states precedence rules.
- **Implications** — highlights requirements (resources, costs, activities) for carrying out the principle; describes impact to business and IT.

**Architecture Repository**
A knowledgebase for all architecture-related projects. Allows projects to manage deliverables, locate re-usable assets, and publish outputs to stakeholders. Contains: Architecture Metamodel, Architecture Landscape, Standards Library, Reference Library, Governance Log, and Viewpoint Library.

**Architecture Requirements Specification (ARS)**
Quantitative statements outlining what a project must do to comply with the defined architecture. Major component of an implementation contract. The ARS directs and controls the creativity of the implementation team and is a companion to the ADD.

**Architecture Roadmap**
Lists individual work packages that will realise the Target Architecture, with a timeline showing progression from baseline to target and the value delivered at each stage.

**Architecture View**
A representation of a system from the perspective of a related set of concerns.

**Architecture Viewpoint**
A specification of the conventions for a particular kind of architecture view. A form of abstraction using selected architectural constructs as structuring rules to focus on particular concerns (ISO 42010).

**Architecture Vision**
A succinct description of the Target Architecture that describes its business value and the changes to the enterprise that will result from its successful deployment. Serves as an aspirational vision, a communication tool, and a boundary for detailed architecture development.

**Artifact**
An architectural work product that describes an aspect of the architecture, consumed or produced by each ADM phase.

**Atomic Business Function (ABF)**
The smallest unit of business function within an enterprise that delivers value. An ABF typically provides services supporting a single enterprise capability.

## B

**Baseline**
A specification that has been formally reviewed and agreed upon, thereafter serving as the basis for further development or change, modifiable only through formal change control. Agile development is high risk without sufficient baseline understanding.

**Building Block**
A (potentially re-usable) component of enterprise capability that can be combined with other building blocks to deliver architectures and solutions. Has a defined boundary and is generally recognisable as "a thing" by domain experts. Types: ABB (requirements, the *what*) and SBB (implementation, the *how*).

**Business Architecture (BA)**
A representation of holistic, multi-dimensional business views of: capabilities, end-to-end value delivery, information, and organisational structure; and the relationships among these and strategies, products, policies, initiatives, and stakeholders.

**Business Capability**
A particular ability that a business may possess or exchange to achieve a specific purpose.

**Business Governance**
Concerned with ensuring that business processes and policies deliver the business outcomes and adhere to relevant business regulation.

**Business Principles, Goals, and Drivers**
Provide context for architecture work. Describe the needs and ways of working used by the organisation.

**Business Scenario**
Used in the Business Architecture phase to identify and understand business requirements that the architecture must address. Describes: business problem, value stream enabled by capabilities, desired outcomes, and actors providing capabilities.

**BDAT (Business–Data–Application–Technology)**
A simple mnemonic for the four primary TOGAF architecture domains.

---

## C

**Capability Architecture**
Provides an organising framework for change activity and development of effective architecture roadmaps realising capability increments.

**Capability Assessment**
Helps to understand the baseline and target capability of the enterprise. Examines capability level, maturity, architectural functions, and gaps.

**Capability Maturity Models Integration (CMMI)**
A proven set of global best practices that drives business performance through building and benchmarking key capabilities.

**Catalog of Frameworks**
Alignment with other major enterprise frameworks required for assurance of outcome and governance. Includes: PMI, PRINCE2, Six Sigma (planning/execution), COBIT, ITIL, Lean (IS governance), Balanced Scorecard, SABSA (management/measurement), and industry-specific frameworks (SCOR, eTOM, BIAN, DoDAF).

**Change Request**
A document submitted to modify current or future architecture work, often used in response to new information such as requirements being unsuitable or insufficient.

**Communications Plan**
A targeted strategy for getting the right information, at the right time, with the right level of detail to the right stakeholders. Based on John Carver's policy governance: should be exclusionary (highlighting what is prohibited rather than what is permitted) and assessed through a reasonable interpretation test.

**Compliance Assessment**
A periodic review completed during implementation to ensure the original Architecture Vision is appropriately realised. Best practice goes beyond compliance with the statement to include compliance with intent. Levels: Irrelevant → Consistent → Compliant → Conformant → Fully Conformant. Non-conformant indicates features implemented contrary to the specification.

**Concern**
An interest in a system relevant to one or more stakeholders. A consistent set of subjects that capture stakeholder interests and consolidate requirements.

**Content Framework and Content Metamodel**
Central TOGAF concepts. The Content Framework describes the types of work products consumed and produced by an EA Capability. The Content Metamodel is a formal description of the architecture including components and their inter-relationships. The Content Framework should be used as a companion to the ADM: the ADM describes *what* needs to be done; the Content Framework describes *what* the architecture should look like once it is done.

**Course of Action**
Direction and focus provided by strategic goals and objectives, often to deliver the value proposition characterised in the business model.

**Critical Success Factor (CSF)**
Defines success for a project. Provides managers and implementers with a gauge for what constitutes a successful implementation.

**Custodian**
A stakeholder with high power and high interest.

---

## D

**Data Architecture**
A description of the structure and interaction of the enterprise's major types and sources of data, logical data assets, physical data assets, and data management resources.

**Deliverable**
An architectural work product that is contractually specified and formally reviewed, agreed, and signed off by stakeholders. Starting point for tailoring.

**DevOps**
An organisational culture that aims to improve the flow of value to customers, focusing on Culture, Automation, Lean, Measurement, and Sharing (CALMS / ITIL® V4).

**DevSecOps**
A mindset where "everyone is responsible for security," with the goal of safely distributing security decisions at speed and scale to those with the highest level of context.

**Digital Transformation**
Fundamentally a strategy and operating model change in which technological advancements are leveraged to improve human experiences, operating efficiencies, and to evolve products and services to which customers remain loyal.

---

## E

**Enterprise**
The highest level of description of an organisation, typically covering all missions and functions. An enterprise often spans multiple organisations. All enterprises seek to improve.

**Enterprise Architecture (EA)**
A strategic tool that presents an approach to identify and address gaps between aspirations and reality. EA addresses stakeholder concerns encompassing business activities and capabilities, information, and technology. EA is communication using artefacts. Gartner: "the process of translating business vision and strategy into effective enterprise change by creating, communicating, and improving the key principles and models that describe the enterprise's future state and enable its evolution."

**Enterprise Architecture Capability**
An ability that an organisation, person, or system possesses to develop, use, and sustain the architecture of a particular enterprise and use the architecture to govern change. Enables measurement of resources employed and outcomes achieved within a specified context.

**Enterprise Architecture Landscape**
Architectural representation of assets in use or planned to use. Complete set of descriptions for the EA. Characteristics: Breadth, Level of Detail, Time, Recency.

**Enterprise Continuum**
A categorisation mechanism for classifying architecture and solution artefacts — both internal and external to the Architecture Repository — as they evolve from generic Foundation Architectures to Organisation-Specific Architectures.

---

## F

**Financial Accounting Model**
The EA leader must identify and document this model for the enterprise. Supports the business model and econometric model. Helps understand how the EA Capability is viewed (cost vs. revenue function, CAPEX vs. COGS vs. OPEX, customer acquisition vs. operational efficiency).

**Foundation Architecture**
Generic building blocks, their inter-relationships, combined with the principles and guidelines that provide a foundation on which more specific architectures can be built.

---

## G

**Gap**
A statement of difference between two states. In gap analysis, the difference between Baseline and Target Architecture. A gap is everything that changes.

**Governance**
The discipline of monitoring, managing, and steering a business (or IS/IT landscape) to deliver the required business outcome. ISO/IEC 38500: a system that directs and controls the current and future state. The key difference between governance and management rests on fiduciary and sustainable responsibility.

---

## I

**Implementation and Migration Plan**
A schedule of projects and programmes for implementation of the Target Architecture. Includes the approach to change management.

**Implementation Governance Model**
Processes, organisations, roles, responsibilities, and measures defined to guide the journey from baseline to target architecture.

**Information**
Any communication or representation of facts, data, or opinions in any medium or form. Information can never be perfect; better quality information enables better quality decisions.

**Interoperability**
In TOGAF ADM, refers to the ability to share operations, data, information, technology, and services across different systems, organisations, and stakeholders.

---

## M

**Metamodel**
A model that describes how and with what the architecture will be described in a structured way.

**Metadata**
Data about data, of any sort in any media, that describes the characteristics of an entity.

**Method**
A defined, repeatable approach to address a particular type of problem.

**Minimum Viable Architecture (MVA)**
Defines the minimum Enterprise Architecture that is realisable and adds business value. Each sprint delivers an MVA.

**Modelling**
A technique through construction of models that enables a subject to be represented in a form enabling reasoning, insight, and clarity.

---

## O

**Objective**
A time-bounded milestone used to demonstrate progress towards a goal (e.g., "Increase Capacity Utilisation by 30% by end of 2024").

**Objectives and Key Results (OKRs)**
Ensure value is being delivered and alignment with standards and regulations. Objectives are qualitative; Key Results are specific, often quantitative performance targets. Set annually, assessed quarterly.

**Organisational Model for Enterprise Architecture**
A chart or document outlining the organisation, roles, and responsibilities within the enterprise. Defines boundaries between EA practitioners and governance relationships.

---

## P

**Partitions**
Lie at the foundation of architecture governance. Distinct from levels and organising concepts of the Architecture Continuum. Allow specific groups of architects to own and develop specific elements of architecture.

**Physical (Architecture)**
A description of a real-world entity. Physical elements in EA may still be considerably abstracted from Solution Architecture, design, or implementation views.

**Project Charters**
Related work packages, business value, risks, issues, assumptions, dependencies, resource requirements and costs, and estimated costs of migration options.

---

## R

**Reference Architecture**
Examples include: IT4IT Reference Architecture (standard information model for IT management) and TM Forum's eTOM and SID (tailored reference architectures for specific organisations).

**Reference Model (RM)**
An abstract framework for understanding significant relationships among entities of an environment and for developing consistent standards or specifications. Aims to become a stable and common language describing functional coverage, baseline (as-is) architecture, target (to-be) architecture, and capability maturity.

**Repository**
A system that manages all of the data of an enterprise, including data and process models and other enterprise information.

**Request for Architecture Work**
Triggers the beginning of an ADM cycle. Sent directly by the Sponsor to the architecture organisation.

**Requirement**
A statement of need that must be met by a particular architecture or work package.

**Requirements Impact Assessment**
Assesses current architecture requirements and specifications to identify changes and their implications when new information has been presented.

**Risk**
Effect of uncertainty on objectives (ISO 31000:2009). A risk assessment determines relevant risks, measures likelihood and impact, and accepts, mitigates, or transfers risk according to the organisation's risk appetite.

**Risk Migration Plan / Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)**
Contains activities to mitigate risks. Implementation of risk mitigation strategy (increase control, transfer, avoid, delay, compensate). ERM reduces uncertainty. Operational risk is the risk a business faces in day-to-day operations based on capabilities produced as the result of EA work.

---

## S

**Security Architecture**
A distinct domain that only exists in reference to other domains and is best considered a concern. Carries two aspects: uncertainty in meeting objectives (risk) and the ability to defend against threats.

**Segment Architecture**
A detailed, formal description of areas within an enterprise, used at the programme or portfolio level to organise and align change activity.

**Service**
A repeatable activity; a discrete behaviour that a building block may be requested or triggered to perform. Security controls are bundled into security services; a security service is an Architecture Building Block (ABB).

**Solution Architecture**
A description of a discrete and focused business operation or activity and how IS/IT supports that operation.

**Solution Building Block (SBB)**
A candidate solution which conforms to an Architecture Building Block (ABB). SBB defines the *how* (implementation).

**Solutions Continuum**
A part of the Enterprise Continuum. A repository of re-usable solutions for future implementation efforts. Contains implementations corresponding to the Architecture Continuum.

**Stakeholder**
An individual, team, organisation, or class thereof, having an interest in a system. Has approval rights in the Target Architecture and decision rights on the suitability of the implementation. Architecture is never *sold* to a stakeholder; stakeholder preferences are never manipulated.

**Statement of Architecture Work (SAW)**
Defines the scope and approach that will be used to complete an ADM cycle. Forms the basis for a contractual agreement between supplier and consumer of architecture services. Used to determine if the project was a success.

**Strategic Architecture**
A summary formal description of the enterprise, providing an organising framework for operational and change activity, and an executive-level, long-term view for direction setting.

**Strategic Fit**
Based upon the overall EA (all tiers), the critical factor for allowing approval of any new project or initiative and for determining the value of any deliverable.

---

## T

**Tailored Architecture Framework**
A customised version of TOGAF and other reference frameworks highly suited to a specific organisation. Tailoring covers: enterprise integration (frameworks, terminology, tools, culture) and specific project use (selecting appropriate deliverables and artefacts).

**Target Architecture**
The description of a future state of the architecture being developed for an organisation. Provides an increase of value at a cost of change.

**Technical Reference Model (TRM)**
A structure allowing the components of an information system to be described in a consistent manner.

**Technology Architecture**
A description of the structure and interaction of the technology services and technology components.

**Trade-off**
A balance achieved between two desirable but incompatible features; losing one quality, aspect, or amount in return for gaining another.

**Transition Architecture**
A formal description of one state of the architecture at an architecturally significant point in time.

---

## V

**Value Stream**
A representation of an end-to-end collection of value-adding activities that create an overall result for a customer, stakeholder, or end user. Value shall be specified from the standpoint of the customer. A value stream shall be identified for each product or service family.

**View**
A representation of the EA Landscape that addresses a set of stakeholder concerns; describes how the architecture addresses those concerns or demonstrates how associated requirements are met.

**Viewpoint Library**
A collection of the specifications of architecture viewpoints contained in the Reference Library portion of the Architecture Repository.

---

## W

**Work Package**
A set of actions identified to achieve one or more objectives for the business. A work package can be a part of a project, a complete project, or a programme.

---

## Extended / Emerging Terms

**Harness Engineering**
The discipline of designing the systems, constraints, and feedback loops surrounding AI agents to make them reliable in production. Involves building infrastructure tools, guardrails, and observability that enables agents to act safely and effectively — shifting focus from merely prompting to engineering the environment.

**Stakeholder Engagement**
Stakeholder engagement is not a soft skill — it is a survival skill for architects. Every major business decision shapes the risk landscape. Executives influence funding and priorities; engagement ensures architecture remains aligned and sponsored.
