# TOGAF 10 — Architecture Skills Framework

> **Purpose:** Defines the skills, roles, and professional development expectations for Enterprise, Segment, and Solution Architects. Use this file to evaluate team capability, define role expectations, assign work packages, and guide architectural career development.

---

## Overview

The TOGAF Architecture Skills Framework (ASF) provides a structured approach to identifying, developing, and deploying the skills required to successfully deliver Enterprise Architecture.

### Categories of Architecture Skills

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Generic Skills** | Leadership, teamworking, interpersonal skills |
| **Business Skills & Methods** | Enterprise organisation knowledge, business cases, business process, strategic planning |
| **Enterprise Architecture Skills** | Modelling, building block design, application high-level design, role definition, architecture principle design, high-level migration planning, ABB management, systems integration |
| **Programme or Project Management Skills** | Managing business change, project management methods and tools |
| **IT General Knowledge Skills** | Brokering applications, asset management, IT system migration planning, SLAs |
| **Technical IT Skills** | Software engineering, security, compute and network infrastructure, data interchange, data management |
| **Legal Environment** | Data protection laws, contract law, procurement law, fraud |

## Architecture Roles

### Enterprise Architect

**Focus:** Enterprise-level business capabilities required across the entire organisation.

**Responsibilities:**
- Architectural design and documentation at the architectural landscape and Technical Reference Model level
- Often leads a group of Segment Architects and/or Solution Architects for a given programme
- Must understand the whole enterprise's business and related IT

**Scope:** Full breadth — business model, all four BDAT domains, governance, and strategic direction

---

### Segment Architect

**Focus:** Enterprise-level business solutions in a given domain (e.g., finance, HR, sales).

**Responsibilities:**
- Architectural design and documentation of specific business problems or organisations
- Re-uses output from Enterprise and Solution Architects
- Joins detailed technical solutions to the overall architectural landscape

**Scope:** A segment or domain of the enterprise. Must understand all architecture domains and related skills, but does not require understanding of the entire enterprise.

> The Segment Architect's skill set is similar to the Enterprise Architect — the difference is scope, not depth.

---

### Solution Architect

**Focus:** System or subsystem technology solutions (e.g., a component such as enterprise data warehousing).

**Responsibilities:**
- Architectural design and documentation at system or subsystem level
- May shield Enterprise/Segment Architects from unnecessary system, product, and technology detail
- More IT-centred than Enterprise or Segment Architects

**Scope:** A specific system or solution. Must understand the business environment where the solution will be delivered. Does not need to be an expert in business modelling or business process design.

**Primary skill domains:** Application, Data, and Technical Architecture.

## Proficiency Levels

Skills are assessed across four proficiency levels (typically):

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Basic understanding; knows the concept and its purpose |
| **Practitioner** | Applies the skill under guidance; developing experience |
| **Expert** | Independently applies the skill; coaches others |
| **Master** | Deep expertise; shapes strategy and standards in this area |

## Solution Architect Responsibilities Checklist

The following responsibilities are expected of a Solution Architect engaged on an architecture project:

- [ ] Align technical strategy with business requirements by following a structured process to gather information, negotiate realistic outcomes, and stay aligned with business goals
- [ ] Develop an architecture design specification accompanied by diagrams. Ensure the specification meets functional and non-functional requirements, including provisions for routine, ad hoc, and emergency operations
- [ ] Create architecture design diagrams illustrating all system design aspects, from broad overview to detailed dimensions (network, identity, etc.)
- [ ] Maintain an **Architecture Decision Record (ADR)** capturing context, consequences, and justifications for architectural decisions. Document trade-offs and rejected options
- [ ] Validate critical assumptions with **proof of concepts (PoCs)**. Before finalising a design, validate high-risk or novel components with working code to prevent theoretical designs from failing in practice
- [ ] Collaborate with workload and platform teams during implementation to provide clarity and recommendations on implementation sequence. Renegotiate requirements with stakeholders if needed
- [ ] Support modelling exercises that provide contextualised information about workload concerns (costs, application health, etc.)
- [ ] Provide **optimisation recommendations** based on observations of usage patterns and changes in workload functionality or cloud provider offerings
- [ ] Participate in audit, compliance, and confidence reviews
- [ ] Act as a consultant during change reviews to provide insight into the estimated cost and feasibility of change

## Emotional Intelligence for Architects

Unlike IQ, emotional intelligence (EQ) develops significantly through experience. High EQ is a key predictor of architectural effectiveness, particularly in stakeholder engagement.

### Five Key Areas

| Area | Description |
|---|---|
| **Self-Awareness** | Understanding your own strengths, limitations, emotions, and their effect on others |
| **Self-Control** | Managing disruptive emotions and impulses; maintaining composure under pressure |
| **Motivation** | Intrinsic drive to achieve beyond external rewards |
| **Empathy** | Understanding the emotional needs of others; building trust with stakeholders |
| **Social Skills** | Managing relationships, influencing, communicating, and collaborating effectively |

## Mentoring Framework

Architecture capability grows through structured mentoring. The following framework guides mentoring relationships within architecture teams.

### Programme Structure
- Minimum 2 meetings, minimum 1 hour per session, over 12 weeks
- Full programme duration: 12 weeks (meeting monthly is ideal)

### Roles

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Mentor** | Empower, don't drive. Model professionalism. Create a safe space. Provide availability, guidance, reflection, and challenge |
| **Mentee** | Lead the relationship. Bring the mindset. Schedule meetings. Take ownership of goals |

### Goal Setting (SMART Framework)
Goals belong to the mentee; the mentor's role is to ask sharp questions and maintain line of sight to goals.

Useful prompts:
- "What would good look like in 4 weeks?"
- "How will we know you've achieved it?"
- "What's realistic given your current workload?"
- "Which step can you do before our next meeting?"

### Goal Levels
1. **Programme-level goals:** Overall outcomes by the end of the programme
2. **Meeting-level goals:** What success looks like for the next session

### Feedback Model: SBI → Feedforward
**Situation → Behaviour → Impact → Feedforward**

### Skills to Develop Through Mentoring
- Goal setting
- Communication
- Growth mindset
- Accountability
- Self-awareness
- Openness
- Building rapport early

## Amazon Leadership Principles — Alignment for Architects

The following principles from Amazon's Leadership Framework align closely with the expectations of TOGAF-aligned architects and are used as a professional expectation benchmark:

| # | Principle | Architecture Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Obsession** | Start with stakeholder and end-user needs; work backwards from value |
| 2 | **Ownership** | Act on behalf of the entire enterprise; never say "that's not my job" |
| 3 | **Invent and Simplify** | Frameworks make systems more manageable, not less complex; find simpler approaches |
| 4 | **Are Right, A Lot** | Seek diverse perspectives; disconfirm your beliefs; strong architectural judgment |
| 5 | **Learn and Be Curious** | Architecture is constantly evolving; commit to continuous learning |
| 6 | **Hire and Develop the Best** | Raise the performance bar; develop architectural talent |
| 7 | **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Architecture standards must be maintained; defects do not get passed down |
| 8 | **Think Big** | Architecture vision must be bold and inspire results |
| 9 | **Bias for Action** | Many architectural decisions are reversible; value calculated risk-taking over paralysis |
| 10 | **Frugality** | Accomplish more with less; constraints breed resourcefulness and invention |
| 11 | **Earn Trust** | Listen attentively; speak candidly; be vocally self-critical |
| 12 | **Dive Deep** | Stay connected to details; be sceptical when metrics and anecdote differ |
| 13 | **Have Backbone / Disagree and Commit** | Challenge decisions when you disagree; once decided, commit wholly |
| 14 | **Deliver Results** | Focus on key inputs; rise to the occasion despite setbacks |
| 15 | **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Build a safe, high-performing, diverse environment; lead with empathy |
| 16 | **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Be humble about the secondary effects of architectural decisions on communities and the environment |

## Architecture Capability Maturity

Architecture Capability is a management concept that facilitates planning improvements in the ability to do something, leading to enhanced outcomes. It enables measurement of resources employed and outcomes or goals achieved within a specified context.

### CMMI Integration
CMMI (Capability Maturity Models Integration) provides a proven set of global best practices to:
- Build and benchmark key architecture capabilities
- Improve performance across architecture domains
- Demonstrate maturity progression to sponsors and leadership

### Four-Purpose EA Capability Model

Effective EA Capabilities serve four purposes:
1. **Operational efficiency** — reducing costs, managing technical debt
2. **Business agility** — enabling rapid response to change
3. **Risk mitigation** — ensuring compliance and managing uncertainty
4. **Value delivery** — ensuring projects realise expected business outcomes

> EA Capability teams that focus on delivering key organisational transformations are statistically more successful than teams that focus primarily on standards, reference architectures, and governance structures.
